### Page 1 - The History of the Asankran Clan

ORIGIN

The Asankran family are said to be descendants of three sisters namely: Kyekye Ampem,
Kyeraa and Ankyaah who are said to have come into existence from the earth under a
certain rock near Akyim Oda. The rock is still worshipped at the place named after the
condition of the place "Oda Aboho".



Kye Boadu and his sister Anima Kyirisuo who was married to an Asante man was barren but
through the help of a fetish priest, Animah gave birth to a female child and named her Emra
Kyereama. Afterwards, Animah became pregnant for a second child and longed for a fish
"Tide" for a meal (As many pregnant woman ask for particular meal) While Animah the
pregnant woman and her maid servant went to fetch the fish there was a heavy thunder
storm which caused the death of Animah. After her death she was referred Animah
Kyeresuo (Kyeresuo: Animah who doesn't like water)



THE JOURNEY

Kyi Boadu, the surviving son of Animah Kyeresuo became the head of the family decided
that they leave the place to look for a more peaceful and fertile land. They were
accompanied by their grandchildren, slaves and a dog to seek for a new settlement.



After a long journey, they were met by Agonahene at Mankaben at Agyakaa. The then chief
of Bogo allowed them to settle on the north known as Gyedua Kankan near the koto stream.



Kye Boadu died at this place and was succeeded by Gyapanyin Kyimah (Rover). Gyapanyin
was a hunter (in view of his work he was given the name Kyimah). One day, he met another
hunter at a place called Aboman and asked the fellow who he was because he did not
expect to meet anybody at that time in the forest. The man answered "I am Bruma Ankomah
Sono Bofoo (I am Ankomah of Bruma, the elephant hunter). Gyapanyin in turn replied that
he was the kyini Bofoo (the roaming hunter) That this juncture they were convince that the
place was a reserve of either of them so they came to terms and took an oath to share the
land between them and made the meeting place the boundary marked by a huge silk cotton
tree. Each of them had agreed to settle on either side of the tree. Here Gyapanyin said he
was making a complete settlement which he expressed "me're sankra ha are" In the course
of time, the people dispersed to cover the whole land.



### Page 2 - The Origin and Establishment of Asankran

THE ORIGIN AND ESTABLISHMENT OF ASANKRAN

"United we stand, divided we fall" The gradual dispersal was the doom of the exiled people,
but it also made the establishment of their name complete which might have been "lost in
transit" through foreign powers or any other adversity, those who went further established
the name Sankran (Staying). The expression "me're Sankra ha are" which means staying
good with Gyapanyin used at Aboman is collectively used for Gyapanyin Kyimah and his
people as the group of people who settled or literally "sankra" at Aboman. They were now
refered to as Asankran-fo (Noun and plural) anywhere they went.



In reality, they are members of the great Kona family which is one of the seven major
families of the Akans in Ghana. It is then, the Asankran family who are the descendants of
the three sisters at Oda Aboho and it is their name (thus family's) that the name of
Asankrangwa is derived.



DISPUTES ON THE STOOL

In later years, Kwadau Sampa, Anyani Buadum, Ditoa Akomia became the rulers of the
district. They befriended Twumasi- believed to have brought a stool from Kancyebo Aowin
and settle at Akyease. Monua Akatro alias Twumasi Kwaagyan (Kwaagyan is the seed of wild
royal palm (adooa). Twumasi on trading grounds as his friends mentioned above had
crossed the Tano river with his younger sister Bombo Asaa. Twumasi asked for shelter and
protection and they were given a certain place known as Akokoammon (A cock doesn't
crow) near Aboabene, a god which is still worshipped.
Bombo Asaa grew to be a beautiful woman and was married (possibly to one of his friends
who had then over powered the natives and had got the stool) a royal and consequently, the
stool went to the children of Bombo Asaa, but the head being Twumasi their uncle. (It is
possible that before Twumasi agreed to give his sister for marriage the stool had been
promised to him because what is highly got is little valued.)



Kruuti of Assin line with his wife a native of Asankran came later. They settled at Ahyire and
gave birth to ten children and that was one of the landmarks in the history of the Asankran
family. The woman was remembered with the sayings "Aframa wo duo nana (Aframa the
mother of ten) It seems to me that when Twumasi and his friends settled there were but an
insignificant hero of the Asankran family really living in that area, otherwise they would have
not been conquered and more evidently Aframah's success of delivering ten children would
not have been so renowned)



### Page 3 - Settlement and Chiefs

These children were driven away and made settlement with Agona family where they lived
at Afiena (Afie-nan) meaning four houses. (it might also mean a settlement of different
people of families) This is probably true.



The children of Aframah moved from Afiena and made their own settlement. They named
this new place after their mother's native town-Oda



Atta Kwado Pisaw rose from these children, picked up arms against Tekyi Brempong who
was a descendant of Bombo Asaa and who was the chief of Asankrangwa. When Atta send
message to Tekyi, there was no reply. In the course of this struggle, Atta died and was
succeeded by Echire who vowed to find out the royal stool and to claim or collect all his
subjects (daughters who had been married and thus left behind) this case was reported to
Nana Ntiako Mensah, Omanhene at Wassa Akropong. A date was fixed for hearing but a war
broke out with the Asantes before the fixed date which bunged the matter at hand.



(I have tried to find out the war with the Asantes but I have not succeeded. It appears it is
the war of 1874 which was according to W.E.F War, the abolishing of slavery)
Note: Immediately after the war with the Asantes, there came about the abolishing of slave
trade (This particular information confirms the truth in my estimation of the date of the war)



Slaves gave men to their masters and left them. Now Ekyire's subjects left and settled at a
place called Tumantu (Tu me a nntu) cannot expel me.



During the period Agyiri, a member of the family who was then through marriage or the

Biyaah the second child of Aframa unfortunately did not bring forth. She was followed by
Appiah who had six children. They were Kruwa, Akua Mrah, Nyaa, Akua Afra, Badu,
Kwakyem. Kruwa brought up Nyarko, Abena Ankora and Nsowah. Akua Nyarko brought forth

Broni who was Kwasi Bedu's uncle occupied the Oda stool but unfortunately died a week

Appianimaa brought up Kokoo, Kuwa and Foriwa. Koko did not get a child. Kuwa gave birth

Opanyin Anyani's line was formerly the Gyase to the Oda stool. Their leader was Epirato. In

Below is a letter from the Asankran Family against the petition of the Asankran-Bremanhene
for the elevation of Breman Stool to Omanhene Status.



Nana Gyapanin Nkyeimah II

Ohene of Oda,

Asankran-Oda,

Post Office Box 22,

Asankrangwa,

31st January, 1990.



RE: PETITION BY ASANKRAN-BREMANHENE

FOR ELEVATION TO THE STATUS OF OMANHENE



We the Chief, elders and people of ASANKRAN-ODA DIVISIONAL AREA wish to refute the
claim of land made by the chief of Asankran-Breman in connection with his petition for
elevation to the status of Omanhene as follows:



1. That Asankran-Oda Divisional Area is a Divisional chief to the Omanhene of Wassa
Amenfi

2. That Asankran-Oda Divisional Area has been in existence a long time ago
3. That Asankran-Oda Divisional Area has co-existed along-side with Asankran-Breman
Divisional Area and that we share a demarcated boundary

4. That Asankran-Oda Divisional Area also has boundaries with Agona Amenfi Divisional
Area, Asankrangwa Divisional Area, and Aowin Paramount Area



The people of ASANKRAN-ODA immigrated from AKIM-ODA to Wassa Amenfi with our
leader NANA KYE BUADU I when Wassa Amenfi Omanhene NANA KROKO ANSAH was then
at NEREBEHE. He directed us to see NANA BRUMANKOMA of BODWESE ANNGU now
AGONA to direct us to settle near the River Tano.



### Page 8 - Settlement and Disputes

On our way to our present settlement, we settled at HWEBI ANSA, then at FE DEE YEYA and
finally we settled at MBOOHO where we named it after our old abode; ODA MBOOHO.



When the people of Breman moved from their former settlement at Chiraa, they
approached NANA KROKO ANSAH at NEREBEHE to find them a new settlement. NANA
KROKO delegated Okyemea Nyame to accompany them to NANA BRUMANKOMA at
BODWESE ANNGU to settle near River Tano to watch against the AOWINS. NANA
BRUMANKOMA asked them to follow NANA KYE BUADU's people who had been settled
near the River Tano.



They first settled at NWAMASE near the present SAA. From NWAMASE, they moved to
NKONTENASE, and then to NSOTOMU from there they went to BETENASE and moved to old
BREMANG.



After their battle with Nana Kojo Ayensu of Asankrangwa which consequently led to the flee
their chief and leader; NANA AMENAMA to SEFWI. At Sefwi, he was captured by the Asantes
and later killed in Kumasi. NANA APPIAH FORI from BREMAN came to NANA KWASI BUAH I,
our ancestor at FE DEE YEYA to seek refuge. He begged to build ONLY FOUR HOUSES. He
was allowed to set it up the hill where he named it Afiana meaning EFIE ENAN (four houses)
In 1919, during the reign of NANA KWASI BUAH KUMA, there arose a boundary dispute
between Oda and Breman at Sekondi High Court where the people of Breman were found
guilty. We have documents to support this.



In 1952, Seven Divisional Chiefs, namely: Breman, Agyakaa-Manso, Agona, Dominase,
Ankassie, Wuratrem and our Stool being represented by OPANIN YAW ADUABAAH signed a
concession Agreement known as DANT SUKUSUKU CONCESSION C.V 487 which was
confirmed by the PARAMOUNT CHIEF OF WASSA AMENFI; NANA KWAME BASSAYIN II



In 1946, Eleven Divisional Chiefs including our stool, then being occupied by NANA KWASI
AMUAH I and Breman Divisional Chief signed the SIKA CONCESSION AGREEMENT which
was confirmed by the PARAMOUNT CHIEF OF WASSA AMENFI.
